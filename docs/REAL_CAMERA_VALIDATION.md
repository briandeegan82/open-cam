# Validating a camera recipe against real captures

`tools/validate_against_capture.py` compares a camera recipe (`config/camera_recipes/*.yaml`) with raw captures from a physical camera. For each measurement it runs a matching simulation with the recipe, using the same signal levels, exposure times, frame counts and ROI sizes. It then reports **measured**, **recipe** and **simulated** values side by side.

The residual to check is *measured vs simulated*. Both numbers go through the same estimator, so the estimator's own biases cancel. These biases include the finite frame count, quantisation, and the EMVA 1288 definition of saturation at the variance peak. For example, that definition puts the measured "full well" a few percent below the recipe's clip level even for a perfect model.

> No real-camera data has been analysed with this tool yet. The tests use captures generated by the simulator itself (`synthesize`). A real validation needs captures from the camera owner.

## Usage

```bash
# round trip on simulator data
venv/bin/python tools/validate_against_capture.py synthesize \
    --recipe config/camera_recipes/default.yaml --out-dir out/capture_synth
venv/bin/python tools/validate_against_capture.py validate \
    --capture out/capture_synth/capture.yaml --out-dir out/capture_synth/report \
    --plot --refit-out out/capture_synth/refit_model.yaml

# real captures (see config/capture_validation/example_capture.yaml)
venv/bin/python tools/validate_against_capture.py validate \
    --capture /data/iphone8/capture.yaml --out-dir out/iphone8_validation --plot
```

Outputs: `report.json` (all numbers, PTC points and MTF curves), `report.md` (tables), `validation.png` (PTC, ColorChecker residuals, MTF), and optionally a re-fitted camera model.

## Data layout

```
capture_dir/
  capture.yaml              # manifest (schema below)
  ptc/dark_t<exp>_<k>.raw16 # dark stacks, >= 2 exposure times
  ptc/flat_l<nn>_<k>.raw16  # flat-field stacks, one level per file group
  colorchecker.raw16
  slanted_edge.raw16
```

`capture.yaml` (schema_version 1; annotated example in `config/capture_validation/example_capture.yaml`):

- `camera_recipe`: the recipe to validate. It can be overridden with `--recipe`.
- `raw`: settings shared by all files. `format` is `raw16` (headerless little-endian uint16, the format `apply_emva_noise.py` writes), `npy`, or `dng`/any LibRaw format (needs `pip install rawpy`; the black level and CFA come from the file). It also holds `width`, `height`, `cfa_pattern` (the 2x2 Bayer tile starting at the top-left pixel of the stored frame, or `null` for mono), `bit_depth`, `temperature_c` and an optional `black_level_DN`. Any section can override `format`, `width` or `height`.
- `emva`: `plane` (R/G/B, default G, meaning the first G site of the tile) and `roi` `[x0, y0, x1, y1]`. `darks` is a list of `{exposure_s, frames}`. `flats` is a list of `{exposure_s, relative_exposure, frames}`, where `frames` is a glob pattern or a list.
- `colorchecker`: `file`, `exposure_s`, and `illuminant` (an id understood by `colour_science.load_illuminant`). Give either `grid` (centres of patch 1 and patch 24 plus `patch_half_size_px`) or 24 explicit `patch_rois`, in row-major order with patch 1 = dark skin.
- `slanted_edge`: `file`, `roi`, `plane`, `exposure_s`, `flatfield` (per-row plateau normalisation for vignetted ROIs), and optionally `simulated_file`.

## Capture protocol

Follow EMVA Standard 1288 Release 4.0 (2021) for the photon-transfer data and ISO 12233:2017/2023 for the edge.

1. **Camera settings.** Use raw output only. Turn off lens shading correction, black-level clamping/dithering, defect correction and noise reduction wherever the camera allows it. Use a fixed analogue gain or ISO, and record it: the recipe's `iso_gain_factor` must match. Let the camera reach thermal equilibrium (at least 15 min powered and streaming) and record the sensor temperature.
2. **Darks.** Cap the lens and work in a dark room. Capture at least 16 frames at each of at least 3 exposure times, including the shortest flat exposure. EMVA recommends at least 100 frames per stack for DSNU; with few frames the residual temporal noise sets the DSNU detection limit. The dark mean vs exposure time gives the dark current, and its intercept gives the black level.
3. **Flats.** Use a uniform, diffuse source, such as an integrating sphere or an opal diffuser in front of an LED. Keep the irradiance non-uniformity across the ROI under 3 %. Use at least 12 signal levels from about 1 % of saturation to clearly beyond it, with points concentrated around saturation. Vary either the exposure time or the source level, and record `relative_exposure` for each level. This is the photon count relative to an arbitrary reference: exposure time multiplied by the source level from a calibrated photodiode. Use at least 2 frames per level (16 recommended). Analyse a central ROI so that lens vignetting does not appear as PRNU; for the PRNU1288 definition use no lens at all.
4. **ColorChecker.** Use an X-Rite ColorChecker Classic under a source whose SPD you know (measure it with a spectroradiometer and add it to `spectra/illuminants/`). Light it at 45/0 geometry with no specular glare. Expose so the white patch reaches 50-70 % of saturation in G. Average several frames or use large patch ROIs.
5. **Slanted edge.** Use a printed or chrome-on-glass edge with moderate contrast (about 4:1 to 10:1, per ISO 12233:2017). Tilt it about 5 deg from vertical, focus carefully, and keep both plateaus at least 10 px wide inside the ROI and unclipped.

## What is compared

| measurement | measured | simulated |
|---|---|---|
| K, read noise, DSNU, PRNU, dark current, saturation capacity, black level | EMVA 1288 PTC on the chosen plane (`analyse_emva`) | `emva_theory.simulate_uniform_stack` with the recipe parameters, at the measured levels, then rounded and clipped to the ADC range, and analysed by the same code |
| ColorChecker per-patch electrons and colour | patch means minus black level, multiplied by the measured K | recipe QE (including IRCF) x illuminant x X-Rite reflectance, scaled so the white patch's G signal matches, pushed through `apply_emva_noise.py` |
| MTF | ISO 12233 SFR on the colour plane (`sfr_analysis.slanted_edge_sfr`) | an edge with the same angle, polarity and per-channel plateaus, blurred by the recipe optics (diffraction at the traced working f-number for 550 nm, plus the `post_psf` Gaussian if enabled), through `apply_emva_noise.py`; or `simulated_file` |

The colour error is a CIEDE2000 difference between measured and simulated values after one shared 3x3 matrix, fitted from simulated RGB to reference XYZ, has been applied to both. It therefore isolates the spectral-response mismatch. It is not the camera's colour accuracy after an ISP. The `white_normalised_error_pct_rgb` per patch and the neutral-patch channel ratios show which QE curve is off.

## Re-fitting a recipe

`--refit-out PATH` writes a full in-file camera model, which `load_camera_model` and `--camera-model-config` accept directly. It is the composed recipe with the measured K, read noise, DSNU, PRNU, black level, dark current (at the capture temperature) and saturation capacity substituted, and with `source.calibration_tier: measured_emva1288`. To turn it into a production recipe:

1. Copy its `sensor`, `noise`, `cfa`, `sensor_forward` and `validation` sections into `config/sensor_models/<camera>_measured.yaml`.
2. Create `config/camera_recipes/<camera>_measured.yaml` with `lens_model:` and `sensor_model: <camera>_measured`.
3. Re-run the validation with the new recipe. The measured-vs-simulated residuals should now be within the estimator noise.

Not re-fitted:

- **QE spectral shape.** The ColorChecker gives only per-channel scale hints (`neutral_channel_ratio_meas_over_sim`). The EMVA 1288 QE measurement needs a monochromator.
- **Optics.** If the measured MTF is far from the diffraction-limited recipe optics, model the lens with a traced prescription in `config/lenses/` and give a pbrt render as `simulated_file`.
- **Row/column FPN, defect pixels and crosstalk.** These are not estimated.

## Limitations

- The simulated EMVA stacks use the closed-form EMVA forward model (`emva_theory`), not every `apply_emva_noise` effect: row/column FPN, defects and crosstalk are left out. Colour and edge simulations run the full `apply_emva_noise.py`.
- Saturation is taken at the variance peak (EMVA 1288). Recipes clip at `full_well_e`, so a matching recipe still shows a measured-vs-recipe "full well" deficit of a few percent. Compare against the simulated value instead.
- With few dark frames, DSNU is limited by residual temporal noise. In the 3-4 frame synthetic round trip, DSNU = 0.3 e- is not resolvable.
- Without `relative_exposure`, simulated flats are matched to the measured means, and saturated levels cannot be reproduced.
- DNG reading through rawpy has not been tested on real files here.
